Dhyutidhar Kitchen — Kukatpally launch blueprint

Prepared 6 September 2026 • All amounts in INR • Owner-operated, delivery-first concept

1. Recommendation

Start with Telangana rice bowls and one chicken dum biryani, serving lunch and dinner from a legally usable 350–500 sq ft kitchen near KPHB. Aim for ₹18,000–₹22,000 monthly rent, with ₹25,000 as a ceiling unless an already fitted kitchen saves substantial setup costs. Begin with a small service area around the actual kitchen, approximately 2–3 km by road where practical, and expand after delivery tests.

Plan ₹8 lakh available capital for a modest independent kitchen: approximately ₹5 lakh to open and ₹3 lakh in operating reserve. A practical planning band is ₹6.5–₹9.5 lakh. A shared, already equipped kitchen could reduce this to roughly ₹3–₹5 lakh including reserve, but no shared-kitchen quote has been verified. Below ₹3 lakh, validate through an appropriately licensed shared kitchen before taking an independent lease. Keep your personal hostel and living expenses separate.

These are estimates, not supplier quotations or guaranteed sales. The plan assumes you manage purchasing, packing, platform operations and accounts full-time, with one cook and one helper. If you cannot do that, add a manager or shift cover and recalculate.

GST: CBIC treats cloud-kitchen cooking/supply as restaurant service, ordinarily 5% without input tax credit under the applicable restaurant treatment. Its e-commerce circular places GST liability for covered restaurant services supplied through the operator on that operator, including relevant unregistered restaurant supplies. This does not automatically exempt your overall business from registration or reporting obligations; platform sales count for applicable aggregate turnover. Ask a CA to determine your position across platform sales, any direct sales and other business turnover, and reconcile fee tax and withholding. 11. Thirty-day validation and launch sequence

Days 1–7 — demand before lease: Interview 30–50 potential customers across several hostels/PGs and apartments, with permission. Ask for their last three actual outside-food purchases, final paid amount, frequency, preferred portion and most common complaint. Audit 15–20 competitors from real delivery addresses at lunch and dinner. Record full checkout cost, portion, ratings/review themes, delivery time and discounts; do not mistake sponsored placement for demand.

Days 8–14 — taste and numbers: Run a ₹10,000–₹15,000 tasting/pilot within the pre-opening envelope using a legally appropriate kitchen. Test six mains with at least 30 paying trial customers, not only friends receiving free food. Measure recipe yield, ingredient cost, packing leaks, food quality after travel and reorder behaviour. Proposed internal gates: at least 80% would buy at the intended final price, 25% actually reorder within a relevant 14-day window, ingredients roughly ≤30% of food subtotal across the mix, and few preventable packing complaints. These are chosen decision rules, not industry statistics.

Days 15–21 — premises and setup: Inspect three properties, obtain written kitchen permission and supplier quotes, decide the final address, complete applicable registrations, standardise recipes and submit platform applications. Start onboarding early; its completion date is not guaranteed within 30 days.

Days 22–30 — controlled launch, if ready: Begin at 15–25 orders/day capacity, then raise to 30–40 as kitchen timing stabilises. Confirm real serviceable addresses before paid ads. Reach stable quality and contribution first; the long-term base-case break-even still requires about 65/day. Launch dates depend on approvals, equipment and platforms.

Review weekly: orders, average food subtotal, ingredient percentage, all platform deductions, contribution/order, refunds, waste, preparation time, repeat rate where measurable, ad spend and cash remaining. If contribution drops below ₹50/order, diagnose discounting, recipe yield, pricing and contract fees before buying more traffic. After 6–8 weeks, if repeat demand and contribution do not improve, shrink the menu, revise the offer or pause rather than increasing fixed costs.
